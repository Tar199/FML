Clustering Method Categorization Advantages Disadvantages Dataset Performance
AGNES Hierarchical (Agglomerative) - It Produces a dendrogram for a visual hierarchy
- With High agglomerative coefficient (~0.92)
- It Catches natural groupings so well		 - Not scalable onto very large datasets
- No automatic selection for those clusters		 Strong
DIANA Hierarchical (Divisive) - Approach from the top aids when some large clusters get expected
- Also capable with dendrograms		 - Lower coefficient from division (~0.83)
- May miss clusters with fine grains		 Moderate
K-Means Partitioning (Centroid-based) - Reasonably efficient and fast
- Simple to interpret
- Suitably works with spherical, convex clusters
- Matches estimated number of wine types		 - Somewhat sensitive to outliers
- Needs k to be defined beforehand		 Best
DBSCAN Density-Based approaches - Able to find arbitrarily shaped clusters without problems
- Detects noise and outliers automatically by itself		 - Requires mostly careful tuning of eps and minPts parameters
- Struggles instead with variable density datasets		 Moderate

A. AGNES

  • Agglomerative coefficient: ~0.94
  • Findings: Clear, nested cluster structures formed as a result. Dendrogram provided interpretable hierarchy.
  • Suitability: Quite useful to explore cluster hierarchy. Works well due to the fully structured and scaled numeric dataset nature.

B. DIANA

  • Divisive coefficient: ~0.80
  • Findings: Displayed distinct divisive tiers, yet AGNES had greater unity.
  • Suitability: Fair. Cluster cohesion is weaker, and the dataset is numerically less optimal than AGNES.

C. K-Means

  • Optimal k: 2 (determined with the Elbow method/WSS)
  • Silhouette score: High (when computed; indicates some strong separation)
  • Findings: Distinctly separated various wine types. Efficient on scaled data. Best visual separation.
  • Suitability: Best choice. The data has numeric values, is well-scaled up, and is roughly spherical in shape — making that ideal for K-means.

D. DBSCAN

  • Eps: 2.2 (chosen according to the kNN distance plot)
  • minPts: 4
  • Findings: Detected multiple clusters as well as noise. Results varied based on parameters. With tuning, there were differences. Less consistent across runs.
  • Suitability: Useful in datasets containing many outliers or non-spherical structures. Comparatively less effective here due to similar densities.

K-Means is largely the suitable clustering method on this wine dataset.

The wine dataset, being fully numeric and standardized, suits distance-based clustering methods. The wine samples seem distributed evenly, with groupings corresponding to cultivars in a natural way. K-Means clustering almost produced well-separated clusters, consistent with expectations from domain knowledge. This method is computationally efficient, but it is also quite easy to interpret. K-Means is an appropriate choice for exploratory data analysis, in light of its performance as well as simplicity. It is also appropriate for practical clustering applications in production.
